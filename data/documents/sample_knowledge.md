# A Short Note on the Apollo Program

The Apollo program was a human spaceflight program carried out by NASA, the
United States space agency. It ran from 1961 to 1972 and had the goal of
landing humans on the Moon.

Apollo 11 was the first mission to land people on the Moon. It launched in
July 1969. Neil Armstrong, an astronaut who worked for NASA, became the first
person to walk on the lunar surface. Buzz Aldrin joined him on the Moon, while
Michael Collins remained in orbit aboard the command module.

The program depended on the Saturn V rocket, a powerful launch vehicle
designed under the direction of Wernher von Braun. The Saturn V was built to
carry the Apollo spacecraft toward the Moon.

# A Short Note on Marie Curie

Marie Curie was a physicist and chemist born in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867. She
conducted pioneering research on radioactivity, a term she coined. Her work
affected the fields of physics and medicine.

Marie Curie worked at the University of Paris. She discovered the elements
polonium and radium. Polonium was named after Poland, her home country. Her
research on radium later contributed to treatments that are used against
cancer.

She won the Nobel Prize twice: once in Physics in 1903, shared with her husband
Pierre Curie, and once in Chemistry in 1911.
